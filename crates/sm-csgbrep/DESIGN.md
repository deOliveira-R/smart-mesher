# sm-csgbrep

CSG deck to B-rep import, checks and oracle (W4 tasks CSG-*): reads an OpenMC-style CSG deck and builds the B-rep model, with the checks and the oracle comparison that validate it. It is a layer-3 crate under the server and must not depend on `sm-server` (the test-only dev-dependency in `tests/mesh_roundtrip.rs` is the one exception). The `csgbrep` plugin is registered in `sm_server::standard()` and registers `model.import_openmc`.

## `model.import_openmc` (W4-CSG-CORE)

One op, one history node (`docs/design/w4/csgbrief.md`). `{geometry, materials?, options?}`; both files are `sm_cadio::Source` values (`{sha256}` read through `StepInputs`, or `{inline}`), resolved by a local copy of the 25-line loader with `csg.*` error codes.

Pipeline (all geometry inside one `topo_txn`, so one `commit_topology`):

1. `sm_csgio::openmc::import` reads the deck into the IR.
2. `deck::place` flattens universes and lattices into cells over canonical global surfaces. It mirrors `sm-csgio`'s `instantiate` (sm-csgio's `ImportedCsg` keeps its surface store private, and the core is frozen) but keeps regions as expressions, so there is no DNF term cap. Surfaces are placed with `SurfaceDef::transformed`, planes and cylinders are canonicalised (a cylinder shifted along its axis is the same surface), a quadric that classifies as a plane or cylinder is replaced by it, and everything else is `csg.unsupported` naming the deck surface (sphere, cone, torus, elliptic: W4-BOOL-TOOLS).
3. `partition::build` cuts a start box (the hull of the cell bounds; ten percent larger on every side that is not an axis-aligned deck plane, so a curved extreme is never tangent to a box face) with `sm_boolean::webcut` (`merge: true`, so every cut face is shared and the result is conformal). A piece is cut by a surface only while some cell that can still own it changes value with that surface (three-valued evaluation of the cell expressions on the piece's sign vector); an untouched piece reports its side, so the sign is exact either way. Planes are cut before cylinders (canonical order), so a lattice splits into boxes first.
3b. Lattice reuse (D4, `reuse.rs`, `op.rs::build_reusing`), in three transactions because `model.copy` opens its own: (a) `build_subset` cuts one prototype element per distinct universe of each reusable lattice (the element at its first slot) and the remainder (the cells outside reusable lattices, with the array boxes reserved: pieces no cell owns there are dropped silently); `commit_topology`; (b) `model.copy` (`TypedOp::apply`) places the other slots as translations of all the prototype's bodies in one call (so the sharing inside the element is kept), in runs along x, then y, then z from slots already placed, the leftovers straight from the prototype; the copies are the live bodies new since the call and are mapped to the cell of their slot by deck origin; (c) merge of the copies, `imprint_bodies` (with merge) of each remainder body against each element body that shares a hull side plane with it (a probe grid just outside the side finds the sides a remainder cell touches), a last merge over everything, then the exterior faces on flagged surfaces (read after the merge, when a face with one volume is exterior).
4. Each finished piece is classified by evaluating the cell expressions: one owner keeps it, none drops it (beyond a one-sided boundary-flagged surface, or outside the bounds: silently; inside: `import.gap` warning with the volume), two or more are an overlap (`csg.overlap` by default; `first_wins` and `report` are options).
5. Pieces of one cell that share a face are united (`boolean::Unite`, breadth-first in ascending order), so the `outer` ring of a lattice is one volume and a cell cut only by planes other cells use stays one volume. A cell that stays disconnected is several volumes in one block.
6. Blocks (`fem.block_add`, numbered from 1 in region-name order, named after the region), sidesets (`fem.sideset_add`: `vacuum` 1, `reflective` 2, `white` 3, on the exterior faces that lie on flagged surfaces), then in one `model_txn` the lattice frames `lat<deck id>` (parents first) and a region tag per volume inside lattice elements, carrying the deck indices (ruling D9).

`TypedOp::apply` of `BlockAdd` and `SidesetAdd` is called with the import's own context (disambiguated from the `Op` blanket impl). The roles they record, `block` and `sideset`, are declared `Many`; the result roles are `deck_volumes` and `deck_bodies` (ascending). The nested `model.copy` records `volumes` and `bodies`: they are declared `Many` and documented as intermediate (the copies, whose faces a later merge changes); consumers read `deck_volumes` and `deck_bodies`.

Diagnostics: `import.partition` (info: kernel cuts, webcut calls, pieces, unions, volumes; work counters, no clock), `import.regions`, `import.gap`, `import.overlap`, `import.reflective_curved`, `import.lattice_rotated`, `import.lattice_multi_placement`.

## Known limits and deviations

- **Lattice reuse (D4) is limited to leaf lattices.** `reuse::plan` reuses a lattice that is placed once, without rotation, has no lattice in its elements, whose element boxes lie inside the enclosing cells (provable from the box corners: planes on one closed side, cylinders with every corner inside) and whose slots of one universe place the same cells. Anything else (nested lattices such as an assembly in a core lattice, a lattice placed several times, a rotated one, one clipped by a curved parent) is cut flat as before; nested reuse (one prototype per assembly universe, itself reusing its pin lattice) is open work for the core decks (D8). A deck where every slot has its own universe also stays flat (nothing to reuse).
- **Cells of the remainder that reach into a reused array** are an overlap (`csg.overlap`, naming `the lattice array`) under the default policy; under `first_wins`/`report` the piece is dropped and the lattice wins. Membership is exact: a remainder piece (owned or not) whose box (vertices and curve samples, enlarged by the largest chord sagitta so a shallow curved straddle between samples is still seen) meets the array box interior is cut by the box planes first, so each part is wholly in or out: the overlap volume of an owned part and the gap volume of an unowned part (reported as `import.gap`, not hidden under the array) are the part's. The margin only makes the test conservative; the cuts settle membership.
- **Ring unite limit (pre-existing, flat too).** Uniting the four pieces of an `outer` ring around an array of 7 x 7 or more (pitch 1 to 1.26) is refused by the kernel (`cannot find a point inside a fragment`); `reuse_lattices: false` fails the same way (pinned in `tests/reuse.rs`). 17 x 17 decks with an `outer` ring therefore wait for the kernel.
- **Flat classification is O(pieces x cells)** (every piece evaluates every cell of its build). Reuse keeps each build small (one element, or the remainder), which is what makes 17 x 17 cheap; a core deck of flat assemblies would still pay it per assembly.
- **Seam turn.** Cylinder tools are cut with their x reference turned by 1 rad about the axis (the surface is unchanged, the canonical form used for ordering and dedup is not). With the default frame the real PWR gap (fuel 0.4096, clad 0.418 to 0.475; clearance 0.0084) fails with `cannot find a point inside a fragment`, the W4-REGIONS-CUT finding; with the turn it imports (`the_real_pwr_gap_imports`, the 17 x 17 test). The cause of the default-frame failure is still open (W4-BOOL-TOOLS).
- **Tangent pin.** A pin tangent to its element box (line contact) is a typed `csg.kernel` error naming the deck surface (`the cut curves branch`), pinned in `tests/import.rs`.
- **Frame names follow the deck lattice id** (`lat<id>`), so renumbering a lattice id moves the state hash; renumbering surfaces, cells, universes and text order does not (`tests/determinism.rs`).
- **Placed-surface dedup** is by exact bits first, then planes (equal normal) and cylinders (equal axis) that differ by at most 1e-7 (below the kernel's linear tolerance) are one surface; the representative is the smaller definition key and the smaller deck id, so text order does not move the hash (`tests/check.rs`). A pin whose clad starts exactly at the fuel radius (two ids, equal radius) therefore has no zero-width cylinder sliver.
- A deck whose cells have no finite bounds needs `options.bounds`.

- **Thin planar pieces (kernel limit, W4-BOOL).** `webcut` of a brick by a plane closer than about 1e-2 (relative to the piece) to a parallel face fails with `result volume invalid` (one plane at x = 0.01 of 10 already does; 0.1 works). The draft's literal CHECK rows (gap 1e-3 in a box of 10, overlap sliver 1e-4) are therefore `#[ignore]`d in `tests/check.rs`, and the same checks run at 0.1 (exact volumes to 1e-9) and 0.5. A curved remainder cell that straddles an array box shallowly cannot be run end to end for the same reason (a radius 100 to 1000 cylinder cut 0.01 to 0.1 from a plane fails in the kernel); the sagitta margin is tested on `meets_interior` itself.

## Oracle against OpenMC (W4-CSG-ORACLE)

Fixtures: `crates/sm-csgio/tests/fixtures/openmc/<name>/{geometry.xml,materials.xml,oracle.json}`, written by `gen.py` (OpenMC 0.16.0, conda env `openmc`; not needed at test time). `gen.py` now resets OpenMC's auto ids per model (a subset run writes the same bytes as a full run; the existing fixtures were generated before and are left as committed, so their ids and the sm-csgio hashes do not move), exits 143 on SIGTERM and records, per point, the lattice elements of OpenMC's own cell path (`lattices: [[id, ix, iy, iz]]`, from `Geometry.determine_paths`). New models: `quadric_cyl` (a quadric that is a z-cylinder off the axes, 0.2 high), `disconnected` (planes, one cell of two parts), `placed_z` (translation and a 35 degree rotation about z), and the W4-REFCORPUS decks copied byte for byte from the corpus (`ref_pin`, `ref_mini3x3`, `ref_assembly17`, `ref_beavrs_assembly`; `SM_REFCORPUS_DIR`) plus `_h2` variants with the z planes moved to +-1 (the kernel cannot cut the 20 cm tall PWR pin yet: `csg.kernel`, deck surface 2; BEAVRS fails at deck surface 36, `cannot find a point inside a fragment`).

Tests:

- `tests/oracle.rs`: membership at the 600 points through `sm_boolean::locate_point` (a bounding-box prefilter, cross-checked against all volumes at every 25th point), the lattice index of OpenMC's path against the region tag (ruling D9; 600 comparisons each in `ref_mini3x3_h2` and `ref_assembly17_h2`), the typed refusal of the tall corpus decks (pinned so a kernel fix shows), and `the_fixtures_are_not_a_vacuous_oracle` (six reader-bug mutations, each must contradict OpenMC at some points).
- `tests/oracle_mesh.rs`: planar chords through the `.smrt` equal OpenMC's to 2e-9 and block volumes are exact; the curved quadric deck stays inside OpenMC's circle and within `C h^2` of it (`C = pi/6` for volume, `1/R` for chords); the all-tet mesh (`mesh.auto {strategy: "tet"}`) of a pin and of a 2 x 2 lattice goes through `mesh.to_poly`, `.smsh` and VTU are written, block volumes agree to 1e-12 and `mesh.validate` is empty; the exports are bit-identical for 1 and many threads. The committed `pin` fixture at gap resolution (600k tets, about 130 s) is `#[ignore]`d.
- Deviations from the draft: (1) the curved mesh is not the inscribed-polygon prism: the tet mesher adds nodes on the cylinder between the circle's nodes (even at a height of one interval), so only the circle bound and the `C h^2` closeness are asserted, not 2e-9 equality with the polygon; (2) `lattice2d`, the corpus decks and every other lattice deck fail in `mesh.auto` (outer-ring face: `trimesh` `points ... coincide`; clad annuli: quality gate), so the polyhedral row uses the generated `pin_lattice` decks (wide gap); (3) `sm_boolean::locate_point` returned `In` for a thin annulus at a point 0.2 outside it (found in `ref_mini3x3_h2`, direction dependent, gone a 0.01 shift away); the bounding-box prefilter hides it from the oracle and the cross-check did not hit it. The polyhedral row ran on the tet mesh only; the hex mesh waits for POLY-MIXED.

## Quadric classifier

`quadric.rs` (W4-CSG-CLASSIFY): the ten coefficients are divided by a power of two near the largest before the Jacobi sweeps, so tolerances hold at any magnitude. Its `classify_with_tol` rustdoc lists the two fail-safe limits (`k_zero` relative to the translation terms only; radius/offset floor about 3e-5).
